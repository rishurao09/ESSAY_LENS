# Dataset Details

This document provides background details on the dataset used to train and validate the Essay Lens classifier.

---

## 1. Composition

The dataset contains a total of 600 admissions-style essays:
- **Human Written (n=300)**: Essays collected from public domain resources, volunteer high school students, and CC-licensed personal narratives.
- **AI Generated (n=250)**: Essays generated by various language models (GPT-3.5, GPT-4, Claude 2) using standard college admissions prompts.
- **Mixed / AI-Polished (n=50)**: Human essays that were polished by GPT-4 to improve flow, grammar, and transitions while maintaining original content.

---

## 2. Splits

The dataset is partitioned into three splits:
- **Train Split (80% / n=480)**: Used to fit standard deviations, means, and logistic regression coefficients.
- **Validation Split (10% / n=60)**: Used to tune decision thresholds and evaluate hyperparameters.
- **Test Split (10% / n=60)**: Held-out completely until the final evaluation to ensure metrics are not overfitted.

---

## 3. Demographics and Subgroups

A key subset of human essays (n=30) consists of essays written by self-identified English as a Second Language (ESL) students. This subset is used to monitor and report ESL false-positive bias.

---

## 4. Known Biases & Limitations
- **Academic Genre**: Since the dataset consists entirely of admissions essays, the model may underperform or yield false positives when applied to highly technical articles, poetry, or casual chat logs.
- **Synthetic Distribution**: The AI-generated samples rely on specific prompt variations; custom prompts that instruct models to vary sentence length or use highly informal vocabulary may successfully bypass the classifier.
- **ESL Writers**: The False Positive Rate for ESL writers is significantly higher (44% vs 11% for native speakers). Automated detection should never be treated as proof of academic dishonesty.
